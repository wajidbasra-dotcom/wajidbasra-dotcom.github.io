Search Architecture

How search and AI retrieval work.

A practical view of the systems between a website and the answer a user sees, from crawling and indexing through traditional ranking and generative retrieval.

01

Website

Accessible pages, clear information architecture, useful content and structured entities.

02

Crawl & Index

Search systems discover URLs, render pages, evaluate signals and maintain indexes.

03

Search & Retrieval

Systems match queries with relevant documents, passages, entities and other sources.

04

Response

Traditional results or generative systems present information for the user's task.

The fundamentals

1. Crawling, rendering and indexing

Search visibility begins with access. Search crawlers need to discover important URLs, fetch their resources and understand the resulting page. Canonicals, internal links, XML sitemaps, robots directives and site architecture all influence how efficiently important content can be discovered and processed.

2. Ranking and SERP features

Once content is available to a search system, ranking systems determine which results and features best satisfy the query. Organic listings are only one surface. Depending on the query, users may encounter local results, images, videos, shopping results, featured snippets and other search features.

3. AI Overviews and AI Mode

Generative search surfaces can synthesize information from multiple sources rather than simply returning a list of documents. The exact systems and interfaces change over time, but the practical SEO implication is stable: clear information, strong entities, accessible pages and useful supporting evidence remain important.

4. Query expansion and retrieval

Complex questions can be decomposed into related sub-questions or retrieval tasks. A system may seek supporting information from different documents and passages before composing an answer. This is why a single keyword page is often insufficient for a complex topic: the site needs a coherent body of relevant, connected information.

5. Source selection and citations

Generative answers need source material. Pages with clear claims, specific evidence, strong topical relevance and unambiguous entities are easier to interpret and potentially useful as sources. This does not mean there is one universal “AI ranking factor”; visibility depends on the search system, query, source set and context.

6. Where SEO fits

SEO sits across the whole chain. Technical work improves accessibility and discoverability. Content work improves relevance and information quality. Internal linking connects concepts. Structured data helps express entities and relationships. Local optimization strengthens geographic relevance. AI Search optimization extends these foundations toward generative retrieval.

Practical takeaway

Do not optimize for an imagined algorithm.

Build pages that are crawlable, understandable, specific, useful and supported by clear first-party information. Then measure what search systems actually expose.
